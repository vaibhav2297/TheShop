# Create Product

## 1. Problem Statement

The Shop's catalogue is what customers browse, filter, and buy from — but nobody can put anything in it. Staff have no way to add a product, and no way to correct one that is already listed: a wrong price, a missing photo, a discontinued flavour all stay wrong. The store also sells products that come in more than one form — the same device in several flavours, the same e-liquid at several nicotine strengths — each carrying its own stock and often its own price, which a single flat product record cannot express. Until staff can create and edit products, with several photos and with the variations customers actually choose between, the catalogue cannot be filled and the store cannot open.

**Solution (one line):** A permission-gated product list from which staff open two forms — add a product and edit a product — where they enter the product's details, upload and order a gallery of images, and optionally define option types (such as Flavour and Nicotine Strength) whose combinations become individually priced, individually stocked variants.

### Scope

**In scope:**
- A minimal product list: a paginated list of every product showing its primary image, name, brand, category, price, and status, and serving as the entry point to both forms.
- An add-product form and an edit-product form, each at its own address, gated by their own permissions.
- Core product details: name, SKU, description, category, brand, price, sale price, stock, and published status.
- An image gallery: uploading several images for one product, reordering them, choosing which is the primary image, and removing images.
- Defining option types with values on a product (e.g. Flavour → Mango, Mint) and having the system produce one variant per combination of values.
- Per-variant configuration: each generated variant carries its own SKU, price, sale price, stock, and availability, and can be pinned to one of the product's gallery images.
- Products without variants: a product can be saved with no option types at all, in which case its price and stock stay on the product itself.
- Retiring the product's dedicated Flavour and Nicotine Strength fields: both are expressed as ordinary option types from now on, making a product's option types the single source of what varies about it.
- Validation, confirmation, and error messages for every field, in English and French.

**Out of scope:**
- Searching, filtering, sorting, and bulk actions on the product list — it is a plain paginated list only, and gains those capabilities in a later feature.
- **Deleting** a product, singly or in bulk. Staff take a product off sale by unpublishing it.
- Creating a category or a brand from inside the product form — both must already exist and be selectable.
- Customer-facing display of variants: the variant picker on the product detail page, how a product with variants is priced in the catalogue beyond showing its lowest price, and building the catalogue's filter controls from the option types this feature makes available (see the cross-feature note in Section 4).
- Bulk product creation: CSV/spreadsheet import, duplicating an existing product, and applying an edit to many products at once.
- Ongoing inventory management: stock adjustments outside the product form, low-stock alerts, stock movement history, and back-orders.
- Scheduled or rule-based pricing (promotions, coupons, time-boxed sales) — this feature sets a price and an optional sale price directly.
- A shared, store-wide library of option types reusable across products; option types are defined per product here.
- SEO fields, marketing copy blocks, related-product links, and an audit trail of who changed what.

### Actors & Access

| Actor | May | Must not |
|---|---|---|
| Staff member with the product-view permission | Open the product list and page through every product, published or not | Add or edit a product unless they also hold that specific permission — those controls are not shown to them |
| Staff member with the product-create permission | Additionally reach the add-product form, fill in details, images, option types and variants, and save a new product | Open a product's edit form unless they also hold the product-edit permission |
| Staff member with the product-edit permission | Additionally open a product's edit form pre-filled with its current details, images, option types and variants, change any of them, and save | Create a new product unless they also hold the product-create permission |
| Staff member without the product-view permission | — | See or reach the product list; a direct attempt is refused with the store's standard access-denied experience |
| Guest / signed-in customer | — | See or reach any part of this feature |

View, create, and edit are each governed by their own fine-grained permission, consistent with the store's access model. Holding one does not imply another, and none is granted by an "is an admin" shortcut.

## 2. Functional Requirements

1. **FR-1:** A staff member with the product-view permission can open the product list and see every product — published and unpublished alike — each showing its primary image, name, brand, category, price, and status.
2. **FR-2:** The product list shows 10 products per page, newest first, with controls to move between pages and an indication of which page is being viewed. It offers no search, filter, sort, or bulk action.
3. **FR-3:** From the list, a staff member with the product-create permission can reach the add-product form, and a staff member with the product-edit permission can open any product's edit form; each control is shown only to the staff member holding its permission.
4. **FR-4:** The add-product form has its own address and opens empty.
5. **FR-5:** A product's edit form has its own address identifying the product and opens pre-filled with everything currently saved: details, images and their order, option types and values, and every variant's configuration.
6. **FR-6:** On either form the staff member enters the product's name and description, and chooses its category and brand from the categories and brands that already exist and are Active. The SKU is generated automatically from the product's name and cannot be typed or edited directly; it stays in step with the name as it changes.
7. **FR-7:** The staff member sets the product's price, and may optionally set a sale price shown to customers as the discounted price alongside the struck-through original.
8. **FR-8:** For a product with no option types, the staff member sets the product's stock quantity on the form; for a product with option types, stock is set per variant instead and the product-level stock field is not offered.
9. **FR-9:** The staff member sets whether the product is Published (visible to customers in the catalogue) or Unpublished (saved but hidden from customers). A new product starts Unpublished so it can be finished before customers see it.
10. **FR-10:** The staff member can upload several images for one product and sees them as a gallery in the order customers will see them. There is no fixed limit on how many images a product may have.
11. **FR-11:** The staff member can reorder gallery images, remove any image, and choose which image is the product's primary image — the one shown on the product card in the catalogue and in the product list. The first image uploaded is the primary until they choose another.
12. **FR-12:** The staff member can define option types for the product, each with a name (e.g. "Flavour") and a list of values (e.g. "Mango", "Mint"), and can add, rename, and remove option types and their values while the form is open. Option types are typed fresh on each product — there is no shared list to pick from — and a product may have as many as it needs.
13. **FR-13:** As soon as option types and values exist, the form shows one variant for every combination of one value from each option type, generated automatically and labelled by its values (e.g. "Mango / 20mg"), together with a running count of how many variants the current options produce. The staff member never adds variant rows by hand.
14. **FR-14:** For each generated variant the staff member sets its SKU, price, optional sale price, and stock quantity, and can mark an individual variant unavailable so it is not offered to customers while the rest of the product stays on sale.
15. **FR-15:** Every SKU is generated automatically and cannot be typed or edited directly: the product's own, derived from its name, and each variant's, derived from the product's SKU and that variant's option values. Each stays live — changing the product's name or a variant's option values regenerates the affected SKU immediately, without a save.
16. **FR-16:** The staff member can pin each variant to exactly one of the product's gallery images, so that choosing that variant shows the pinned image first. A variant with no pinned image falls back to the product's primary image. When a product has more than one option type, the pin control also offers to apply the chosen image to every variant sharing one of that variant's option values — for example every variant with Flavour = Mango — so an image that distinguishes one option value is pinned once rather than once per combination.
17. **FR-17:** Adding, removing, or renaming an option value updates the variant list in place: existing variants keep the prices, stock, image and availability already entered (their SKU regenerates to match a renamed value, per FR-15), variants for new combinations appear with an automatically generated SKU and no other values, and variants whose combination no longer exists are dropped after the staff member confirms.
18. **FR-18:** A product may be saved with no option types at all. Such a product has no variants, and its single price and stock apply directly.
19. **FR-19:** When a product has variants, the product's own price field is no longer what customers pay; the form makes clear that customers see the range starting at the lowest variant price and pay the price of the variant they choose.
20. **FR-20:** A product no longer carries a dedicated Flavour or Nicotine Strength field. Both are expressed as option types like any other variation, so what varies about a product — and the values customers can choose between — comes from that product's own option types.
21. **FR-21:** The staff member can save the form at any point while the product is Unpublished, even with parts still unfinished, so a product can be built up over more than one sitting. Publishing requires the product to be complete.
22. **FR-22:** After a successful save the staff member sees a confirmation naming the product, stating whether it is Published or Unpublished, and returns to the product list, which reflects the change.
23. **FR-23:** Invalid input is rejected with clear, field-specific messages against the offending fields — including individual variant rows — and everything already entered, including uploaded images, is preserved so the staff member can correct and resubmit.
24. **FR-24:** Attempting to leave either form with unsaved changes warns the staff member that their changes will be lost and lets them stay.
25. **FR-25:** Images the product no longer uses — removed while editing, or belonging to a product that was never saved — are discarded rather than left stored.
26. **FR-26:** A staff member without the relevant permission is not shown the capability, and a direct attempt to reach the list or either form is refused with the store's standard access-denied experience.
27. **FR-27:** All text in this feature — headings, column and field labels, help text, option and variant table labels, buttons, confirmations, validation messages, empty-state text, and access-denied messages — is available in English and French and follows the active site language.

## 3. Functional Behaviors

### Behavior 1: Browse the product list
- **User does:** A staff member with the product-view permission opens the product list.
- **User sees:** The 10 most recently added products, each with its primary image, name, brand, category, price, and Published or Unpublished status, plus pagination controls. An "add product" control appears only if they hold the create permission, and a way into each product's edit form only if they hold the edit permission.

### Behavior 2: Add a simple product with no variants
- **User does:** A staff member with the product-create permission opens the add-product form from the list, enters a name and description — the product's SKU is generated automatically from the name as they type — picks a category and a brand, sets a price and a stock quantity, uploads two images, sets the status to Published, and saves.
- **User sees:** A confirmation naming the product and stating it is Published, and a return to the product list which now includes it. The product is in the customer-facing catalogue, showing its primary image, name, and price.

### Behavior 3: Build the image gallery
- **User does:** Uploads several images at once, drags one to the front, marks a different one as primary, and removes another.
- **User sees:** Each image appears in the gallery as it uploads, with a clear marker on the primary image and the order shown as customers will see it. Removing an image takes it out of the gallery immediately; if it was primary, the first remaining image becomes primary.

### Behavior 4: Define option types and get variants
- **User does:** Adds an option type "Flavour" with values Mango and Mint, then an option type "Nicotine" with values 20mg and 50mg.
- **User sees:** Four variants appear — Mango/20mg, Mango/50mg, Mint/20mg, Mint/50mg — each already carrying an automatically generated SKU and waiting for a price and stock, with the count of variants shown. The product-level stock field disappears and a note explains customers will see a price range starting at the lowest variant price.

### Behavior 5: Configure each variant
- **User does:** Fills in a price and stock for each variant (their SKUs are generated automatically from the product's SKU and each variant's own option values, with no field to type over them), sets a sale price on one, pins the Mango photo from the Mango / 20mg row and applies it to every Flavour = Mango variant, does the same for Mint, and marks one variant unavailable.
- **User sees:** Each row shows what was entered, the pinned image as a thumbnail on its row, and the unavailable variant visibly marked as not offered to customers. Two pinning actions covered all four rows. Rows still missing required information are flagged.

### Behavior 6: Change the options after variants are configured
- **User does:** Adds a third flavour, "Berry", then removes the value "20mg".
- **User sees:** Two new Berry rows appear with automatically generated SKUs while every already-configured row keeps its price, stock, image, and availability, and the variant count updates. Removing "20mg" first asks for confirmation, naming how many configured variants will be discarded; after confirming, those rows are gone and the rest are untouched.

### Behavior 7: Edit an existing product
- **User does:** A staff member with the product-edit permission opens a product's edit form from the list, changes the price of one variant, adds an image, and saves.
- **User sees:** The form opened showing exactly what was saved before. After saving, a confirmation naming the product and a return to the list; the change is reflected wherever the product appears in the store.

### Behavior 8: Save an unfinished product as a draft
- **User does:** Enters a name — its SKU is generated automatically — picks a category and a brand, leaves the price, images, and variants unfinished, keeps the status Unpublished, and saves.
- **User sees:** A confirmation that the product was saved as Unpublished, with a note that it is not visible to customers, and the product listed as Unpublished. Reopening its edit form shows everything exactly as left.

### Behavior 9: Try to publish an incomplete product
- **User does:** Sets the status to Published on a product with no images and one variant missing its price, and saves.
- **User sees:** The save is refused with a message listing what is missing to publish, with the offending fields and variant rows flagged; nothing entered is lost, and the product stays Unpublished if it was.

### Behavior 10: Fix invalid input
- **User does:** Saves with the name blank, a sale price higher than the price, and two option values ("Mango!" and "Mango?") that generate the same SKU once slugified.
- **User sees:** Each problem is reported against its own field or variant row in plain language; the images, options, and every other entered value are still there.

### Behavior 11: Attempt a capability without permission
- **User does:** A staff member holding only the product-view permission follows a direct link to the add form; a user with no product permission at all follows a direct link to the product list.
- **User sees:** The store's standard access-denied experience in both cases; the capability never appeared to them.

## 4. Constraints

- This is an admin-panel feature. View, create, and edit are each gated by their own fine-grained permission in line with the store's access model — never by an "is an admin" shortcut and never by a single blanket "manage products" permission.
- The product list shows 10 products per page, newest first; the page size and order are fixed and not chosen by the staff member.
- Prices are entered and shown in Canadian dollars (CAD) using the site's currency format, in both languages.
- A product's name and description are stored exactly as the staff member types them and are not translated — one description is entered, with no separate English and French versions. All of the feature's own interface text is available in English and French.
- Option type names and values are likewise entered once, untranslated, and are shown to customers as typed.
- Images must be of an accepted type and within a size limit: PNG, JPG, and WebP up to 2 MB each — the same limits the store applies to a brand logo and a category image. No limit is placed on how many images, option types, or variants a single product may have.
- A product's edit address identifies the product by its own permanent identifier, so a saved link keeps working after the product is renamed.
- No product is ever deleted by this feature; a product taken off sale is Unpublished and stays in the admin.
- Only Active categories and Active brands can be chosen for a product. A product already assigned to a category or brand that has since been deactivated keeps that assignment and can still be saved.
- **Cross-feature dependency:** making option types the only home for flavour, nicotine strength, and every other variation means the customer catalogue can no longer filter on a fixed pair of built-in fields. The catalogue must offer filters built from the option types and values that products actually carry. This feature supplies that information; changing the catalogue's filter controls to read from it belongs to the product-catalogue feature and must be picked up there.
- The product list, both forms, the image gallery (including reordering and choosing the primary image), the option and variant tables, and every confirmation prompt are fully operable by keyboard with a visible focus indicator; validation messages are announced to screen-reader users and clearly associated with their field or variant row; upload progress and completion, and changes to the variant list caused by editing options, are announced; and each confirmation prompt takes focus when it opens and returns focus where it was raised from when dismissed.

### Business Rules

| ID | Rule | Outcome when violated |
|---|---|---|
| **RULE-1** | A product name is required and must contain at least one visible character; it must not exceed 150 characters. A description is optional and must not exceed 2000 characters. | The save is rejected with a message naming the offending field and its limit; nothing is created or changed and the entered input is preserved. |
| **RULE-2** | A product name is unique across the catalogue, ignoring capitalization and leading/trailing spaces — no two products may share a name. Because the SKU is generated automatically from the name, unique names are what keep those generated SKUs from colliding (RULE-8) — except for the rare case where two different names slugify to the same text, which still falls back to the ordinary SKU-conflict refusal. | The save is rejected with a message on the name field saying a product with that name already exists; nothing is created or changed and the entered input is preserved. |
| **RULE-3** | A category and a brand are both required, and each must be chosen from those that are currently Active. | The save is rejected with a message that a category and a brand must be selected; inactive categories and brands are never offered to choose from. |
| **RULE-4** | A price is required for a product with no option types, must be greater than zero, and is recorded to at most two decimal places. | The save is rejected with a message that a valid price is required. |
| **RULE-5** | A sale price, wherever it may be entered (on the product or on a variant), is optional; when given it must be greater than zero and strictly less than the corresponding original price. | The save is rejected with a message that the sale price must be lower than the price, against the field or variant row that carries it. |
| **RULE-6** | Stock is a whole number of zero or more. A product with no option types carries one stock quantity; a product with option types carries one per variant and none of its own. | A negative or fractional quantity is rejected with a message stating stock must be a whole number of zero or more. |
| **RULE-7** | Every image must be of an accepted type and within the size limit, and exactly one image is the primary image whenever any image exists. A product may hold as many images as the staff member uploads — there is no maximum. | An image of the wrong type or too large is refused with a message stating the accepted types and size limit; the rest of the form is untouched. |
| **RULE-8** | Every product must have a SKU, and so must every variant. All of them share one namespace: a SKU is unique across the entire catalogue — against every other product's and every other variant's — ignoring capitalization and leading/trailing spaces. Every SKU is generated automatically (RULE-15) and cannot be typed or edited directly; a SKU already in use is refused however it arose. A product and its variants keep their own SKUs when the product is edited without a change to the product's name or the affected option values; changing either regenerates the SKUs that are derived from it. | The save is rejected, naming the product field or the variant rows whose SKU is missing or already in use, and naming the conflicting SKU; nothing is created or changed. |
| **RULE-9** | Each option type needs a name and at least one value; option type names are unique within the product and values are unique within their option type, both ignoring capitalization and surrounding spaces. Option types belong to the product that defines them — the same name on two products means nothing shared — and a product may define as many as it needs. | The offending option type or value is rejected with a message saying it is duplicated or empty; no variant is generated from it. |
| **RULE-10** | The variant list is always exactly one variant per combination of one value from each option type — never more, never fewer, and never edited by hand. The number of variants the current options produce is shown to the staff member as they edit, and is not capped. | Not violable by the staff member; the variant list can never disagree with the option types that produced it. |
| **RULE-11** | Removing an option type or one of its values discards every variant built from it, together with that variant's SKU, prices, stock, pinned image, and availability. The gallery images themselves are untouched — removing an option type or value never removes an image from the product. It only happens after the staff member confirms a prompt stating how many configured variants will be discarded. | Without confirmation nothing is discarded; dismissing the prompt leaves the options and variants exactly as they were. |
| **RULE-12** | Adding an option value never disturbs an existing variant: every variant that survives the change keeps everything already entered against it. | Not violable by the staff member; re-generating the variant list never silently clears configured rows. |
| **RULE-13** | A variant carries at most one pinned image, and it must be one of the product's own gallery images. Pinning decides which image is shown first for that variant; it never narrows the gallery, so every image a product has stays reachable whichever variant is selected. Removing a gallery image that variants are pinned to leaves those variants unpinned, falling back to the primary image. | Not violable by the staff member; a variant can never point at an image the product does not have, carry more than one, or hide the rest of the gallery. |
| **RULE-14** | To be Published a product must have a name, a category, a brand, at least one image, and — if it has option types — every variant complete with a SKU, a price, and a stock quantity; a product with no option types must additionally have a price and a stock quantity. | The save is refused with a message listing everything missing, each flagged against its field or variant row; the product's status is unchanged. |
| **RULE-15** | An Unpublished product can be saved at any level of completeness, provided every value that *has* been entered is itself valid (RULE-1 to RULE-13). The exceptions are the four values required at every save, complete or not: a name, a SKU, a category, and a brand (RULE-1, RULE-3, RULE-8). | An invalid entered value is rejected on its own merits even when saving as Unpublished; a merely absent value is not, unless it is one of those four. |
| **RULE-16** | A published product with option types is available to customers when at least one of its variants is both marked available and has stock above zero; a published product with no option types is available when its own stock is above zero. | Not violable by the staff member; a product all of whose variants are unavailable or out of stock is shown to customers as out of stock rather than hidden. |
| **RULE-17** | An Unpublished product is not shown to customers anywhere in the store — not in the catalogue, its filters, or search. It remains fully visible and editable to staff, and appears in the product list marked Unpublished. | Not violable by the staff member; unpublishing never deletes a product or its images. |
| **RULE-18** | An image the product no longer uses — removed while editing, replaced, or uploaded to a product that was then abandoned without saving — is discarded rather than kept. | Not violable by the staff member; no unused image is ever left stored. |
| **RULE-19** | Flavour, nicotine strength, and every other variation are expressed only as option types. No product carries a separate flavour or nicotine field, and the values customers can filter and choose between are exactly the option values products define. | Not selectable: the forms offer no dedicated flavour or nicotine field. A product that should vary by flavour and has no Flavour option type simply has no flavour to show or filter on. |
| **RULE-20** | Each capability is checked against its own permission at the moment it is used, not only when the page is drawn. | A list or form reached without its permission — including by direct link — is refused with the standard access-denied experience. |

## 5. Edge Cases & Error Handling

- **Edge case:** No products exist yet → **User experience:** The list shows an empty-state message explaining no products have been added, with the add-product option if they hold that permission.
- **Edge case:** The product list is still loading → **User experience:** A loading indication is shown in place of the list, so it never looks like an empty catalogue.
- **Edge case:** No Active categories or no Active brands exist yet → **User experience:** The relevant chooser shows an empty-state message explaining one must exist first and pointing to where categories or brands are managed; the product cannot be saved until one is chosen (RULE-3).
- **Edge case:** The staff member uploads a file that is not an accepted image type or is too large → **User experience:** That file is refused with a message stating the accepted types and size limit; every other image and every entered value is untouched (RULE-7).
- **Edge case:** An image upload fails part way → **User experience:** That image is shown as failed with a way to retry or dismiss it; the rest of the gallery and the form are unaffected, and the failed image is not counted toward the product.
- **Edge case:** The staff member removes the image that is currently primary → **User experience:** The next image in the gallery becomes primary automatically and is marked as such; if it was the only image, the product has no primary image and cannot be published until one exists (RULE-7, RULE-14).
- **Edge case:** The staff member removes a gallery image that variants were pinned to → **User experience:** Those variants show as using the product's primary image again, with a brief note explaining why; no variant is otherwise changed (RULE-13).
- **Edge case:** The staff member removes an option type or value whose variants were pinned to gallery images → **User experience:** Those variants are discarded after the usual confirmation, but every image stays in the gallery in its existing order, ready to be pinned again by another variant (RULE-11).
- **Edge case:** The staff member pins an image and applies it to every variant sharing an option value, then changes one of those variants individually → **User experience:** Only that variant changes; the others keep the image applied to them, since each variant holds its own pin rather than sharing one (FR-16).
- **Edge case:** The option values entered produce a very large number of variants → **User experience:** The running variant count makes the size clear before any row is filled in, and the variant list stays navigable; nothing is refused on grounds of size (RULE-10).
- **Edge case:** The staff member removes an option value whose variants are already fully configured → **User experience:** A confirmation names how many configured variants will be discarded and warns the entered SKUs, prices, and stock are lost; cancelling leaves everything as it was (RULE-11).
- **Edge case:** The staff member removes the last option type from a product that had variants → **User experience:** After the same confirmation, all variants are discarded, the product-level price and stock fields reappear, and the staff member must supply them before publishing (FR-18, RULE-11).
- **Edge case:** The staff member enters a product name another product already uses → **User experience:** The save is refused with a message on the name field saying that name is taken; everything else entered is preserved (RULE-2).
- **Edge case:** A SKU on the form — the product's own or a variant's, always arrived at automatically — is already used elsewhere in the catalogue, or two rows on this form carry the same one (typically two option values that slugify to the same text) → **User experience:** The save is refused with the offending field or variant rows flagged and a message naming the conflicting SKU; every other entry is preserved, and since no SKU can be typed directly, the staff member resolves it by changing the product's name or the colliding option value and saves again (RULE-8).
- **Edge case:** The staff member marks every variant unavailable, or sets every variant's stock to zero, and publishes → **User experience:** The save succeeds; the product appears to customers as out of stock rather than disappearing (RULE-16).
- **Edge case:** The staff member sets a sale price equal to or higher than the price, on the product or on a variant → **User experience:** The save is refused with a message on that specific field saying the sale price must be lower than the price (RULE-5).
- **Edge case:** The staff member tries to publish a product with no image, or with a variant missing its price → **User experience:** The save is refused with a list of everything missing to publish, each flagged in place; the product stays as it was (RULE-14).
- **Edge case:** The staff member navigates away, closes the form, or follows a link with unsaved changes → **User experience:** A warning says the changes will be lost and offers to stay; leaving anyway discards the changes and any images uploaded for the unsaved product (FR-24, RULE-18).
- **Edge case:** The product being edited was changed by another staff member in the meantime → **User experience:** A clear message says the product has changed since it was opened, and the staff member is shown the current state rather than silently overwriting it.
- **Edge case:** The category or brand a product is assigned to has since been deactivated → **User experience:** The edit form still shows the current assignment, marked as inactive, and the product can be saved without reassigning it; choosing a *different* category or brand only offers Active ones.
- **Edge case:** A save fails for a reason outside the staff member's control (e.g. a connection problem) → **User experience:** A clear message says the product was not saved and invites them to try again; everything entered — including uploaded images, options, and variant rows — is preserved, and the product is left exactly as it was, never partly saved.
- **Edge case:** The edit form is still loading a product's details, images, and variants → **User experience:** A loading indication is shown in place of the form, and no field appears empty-but-editable before its saved value arrives.
- **Edge case:** A staff member follows a saved edit link for a product that no longer exists → **User experience:** A clear message says the product could not be found, with a way back to the product list.
- **Edge case:** A staff member holds view but not create or edit → **User experience:** They can browse the whole list, but no add control and no way into an edit form is shown, and a direct attempt at either is refused with the standard access-denied experience (FR-26, RULE-20).

## 6. Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] **AC-1:** Given a staff member with the product-view permission and more than 10 products in the store, when they open the product list, then they see the 10 most recently added products — published and unpublished alike — each with its primary image, name, brand, category, price, and status, together with pagination controls and no search, filter, sort, or bulk-action control. Verifies FR-1, FR-2, RULE-17.
- [ ] **AC-2:** Given the product list spans multiple pages, when the staff member moves to the next page, then they see the next set of up to 10 products in the same newest-first order. Verifies FR-2.
